How the Online Metronome Works

A metronome produces evenly spaced clicks at a tempo you choose, measured in beats per minute. At 60 BPM there is exactly one click every second; at 120 BPM there are two clicks per second. Practising against that fixed pulse trains your internal sense of time so you stop rushing fast passages and dragging slow ones.

A common practice method is to learn a tricky passage slowly and cleanly, then raise the tempo in small steps of about 5 BPM only once you can play it without mistakes. This gradual approach builds accuracy first and lets real speed follow naturally, which is far more effective than simply trying to play fast. Tempo markings on sheet music map to BPM ranges: for example Largo sits around 40–60 BPM, Andante around 76–108 BPM, and Allegro around 120–168 BPM, so a metronome lets you match the composer's intended pace exactly.

Example: to practise a piece marked Moderato, set the metronome near 100 BPM and only speed up once the passage is clean.
Example: a guitarist working up a solo starts at 80 BPM, nails it, then nudges to 85, 90 and 95 BPM in stages until reaching the target tempo.

What is a good BPM to practise at?
